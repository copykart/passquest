## The Real Reason Indian Candidates Score Band 6 on Writing Task 2

To understand the problem, you first need to understand how Writing Task 2 is actually marked.

IELTS examiners do not read your essay holistically and assign a general impression score. They score it against four separate, equally-weighted criteria. Each criterion is worth 25 percent of your Writing Task 2 mark.

### The Four Band Descriptor Criteria

<div class="table-wrap">

| Criterion | What It Measures | What Band 7 Requires |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------------|
| **Task Achievement** | Did you answer the question? Is your position clear and fully developed? | Clear central position with fully developed main ideas and relevant, extended support |
| **Coherence and Cohesion** | Does the argument flow logically? Are ideas connected skilfully? | Information logically organised, progression clear throughout, cohesive devices used flexibly |
| **Lexical Resource** | Range and accuracy of vocabulary | Sufficient range for some flexibility and precision, less common items with only occasional inaccuracies |
| **Grammatical Range and Accuracy** | Variety and correctness of sentence structures | A variety of complex structures, frequent error-free sentences, good control of grammar and punctuation |

</div>

Most Indian candidates score well on **Task Achievement** — they answer the question and develop a clear position. They score reasonably on **Lexical Resource** — Indian academic education produces a solid vocabulary range. They score well on **Grammatical Range and Accuracy** — Indian English education places heavy emphasis on grammar.

They consistently score **Band 5–6 on Coherence and Cohesion.**

And because all four criteria are equally weighted, a Band 5 on Coherence and Cohesion pulls the entire Writing Task 2 score down to Band 6 — regardless of how strong the other three criteria are.

## The Coherence and Cohesion Problem: Why It Happens to Indian Students

Coherence and Cohesion assesses two related things:

**Coherence** — whether your argument flows logically from one idea to the next, whether your paragraphs develop progressively, and whether your overall essay has a clear and consistent direction.

**Cohesion** — the specific devices you use to connect ideas: pronouns, synonyms, subordinate clauses, referencing, and the skilful use of discourse markers.

The reason Indian candidates consistently lose marks on this criterion is not lack of knowledge. It is a specific pattern that is taught — deliberately — in Indian academic writing education:

**The template structure with discourse markers.**

Most Indian students are taught to write structured essays using the following sequence:

*Introduction → Firstly, [point 1] → Secondly, [point 2] → Furthermore, [point 3] → In conclusion, [summary]*

This structure is logical. It is organised. It is clear. And it is scored at Band 5–6 for Coherence and Cohesion by IELTS examiners because it is **formulaic**.

The band descriptors for Coherence and Cohesion at Band 7 specifically require cohesive devices to be used **flexibly and with only occasional lapses.** At Band 6, they describe a candidate who **uses cohesive devices effectively, but there is some under- and over-use.** The over-use of Firstly, Secondly, Furthermore is exactly what the Band 6 descriptor is describing.

Examiners who mark hundreds of essays per week recognise this template immediately. It signals a candidate who knows how to follow a formula but has not demonstrated flexible, independent argument construction.

## What Band 7 Coherence and Cohesion Actually Looks Like

Here is the same argument written at Band 6 and Band 7 Coherence and Cohesion level — using the same ideas and similar vocabulary.

### Band 6 Version — The Template

*"There are several reasons why remote work has become popular. Firstly, it allows employees to save time on commuting. Secondly, it gives workers more flexibility in managing their personal lives. Furthermore, companies can reduce office costs significantly. In conclusion, remote work benefits both employees and employers in multiple ways."*

**What the examiner sees:** Formulaic structure. Discourse markers used mechanically. No logical connection between ideas — each point is simply listed and labelled. The argument does not develop or build.

### Band 7 Version — Flexible and Logical

*"The rise of remote work reflects a fundamental shift in how both employees and employers understand productivity. Workers who previously spent hours commuting can redirect that time toward their output — a change that benefits not only their wellbeing but also the quality of their work. This efficiency gain extends to employers as well, since reduced office requirements translate directly into lower operational costs. What began as a response to circumstance has evolved, for many organisations, into a strategic advantage."*

**What the examiner sees:** Ideas connected logically without mechanical labelling. Pronouns and synonyms (*this efficiency gain*, *that time*, *a response to circumstance*) doing the cohesive work rather than discourse markers. The argument builds from one idea to the next rather than listing separate points.

**The key difference:** The Band 7 version does not tell you the structure. It shows you the logic. The reader follows the argument because the ideas connect, not because they have been labelled with *Firstly* and *Secondly*.

## The Three Specific Fixes for Indian IELTS Candidates

### Fix 1: The Discourse Marker Ban

For your next three Writing Task 2 practice essays, ban the following words entirely:

- Firstly / Secondly / Thirdly
- Furthermore / Moreover / In addition
- However (as a sentence opener — it can be used mid-clause)
- In conclusion / To conclude / To summarise

These words are not inherently wrong. They are wrong when they are the **only** cohesive tool you use. By banning them temporarily, you force yourself to find other ways to connect ideas — pronouns, synonyms, subordinate clauses, logical sequencing.

After three essays without these words, reintroduce them selectively. One discourse marker per paragraph maximum. Varied — not the same one repeated.

### Fix 2: The Backwards Paragraph Check

After writing your essay, read it backwards — paragraph by paragraph, not sentence by sentence.

Start at your conclusion. Read it. Ask: does this follow logically from the body paragraph above it?

Read your final body paragraph. Ask: does this follow logically from the paragraph above it?

Continue to the introduction.

Any paragraph where the answer is "not really" or "sort of" contains a coherence gap. The ideas in those paragraphs are not connected — they are simply placed adjacent to each other. Fix the logical connection and your Coherence score improves immediately.

### Fix 3: The Synonym Chain

Take your Task 2 topic — for example, "the increase in urbanisation." Map out the key nouns in your argument and write three synonyms or related phrases for each before you start writing:

- *urbanisation → urban growth, the expansion of cities, the movement of populations to urban areas*
- *challenge → problem, pressure, consequence, difficulty*
- *government → policymakers, authorities, officials, the state*

Using these chains throughout your essay does two things simultaneously: it demonstrates Lexical Resource and it creates Cohesion through varied reference — which is exactly what Band 7 Coherence and Cohesion requires.

## The Task Achievement Trap Indian Students Also Fall Into

While Coherence and Cohesion is the primary gap for most Indian candidates, there is a secondary Task Achievement error that is specific to Indian academic writing training.

**The error: arguing all sides without taking a position.**

Indian academic writing education often values balance — presenting multiple perspectives and letting the reader decide. This is excellent academic writing. It is not what IELTS Writing Task 2 rewards.

IELTS Task 2 requires you to take a **clear position** and develop it fully. When a question asks "To what extent do you agree?" the examiner expects you to state clearly whether you agree, disagree, or partially agree — and then develop that position throughout the entire essay.

Indian candidates who write "On one hand… on the other hand… it is difficult to say which is more important" consistently lose Task Achievement marks because their position is not clear or consistent.

**The fix is simple:** Decide your position before you start writing. Write it in your introduction. Develop it in your body paragraphs. Confirm it in your conclusion. Every paragraph should be working to support your stated position — not presenting alternative views that undermine it.

## The IELTS Writing Task 2 Structure That Consistently Scores Band 7

This is not a template. It is a logic structure. The difference matters.

**Introduction (50–70 words)**
- Paraphrase the question topic (do not copy the exact wording — this scores zero for Lexical Resource)
- State your clear position on the question
- Preview the two or three main ideas you will develop

**Body Paragraph 1 (80–100 words)**
- State your first main idea
- Explain why it is true (the mechanism, not just the assertion)
- Give one specific, relevant example (real or plausible)
- Connect to your overall position

**Body Paragraph 2 (80–100 words)**
- State your second main idea
- Explain and develop it
- Give one specific, relevant example
- Connect logically to Body Paragraph 1 and your overall position

**Body Paragraph 3 (optional, 60–80 words)**
- A concession or counter-argument if your question requires it
- Brief — this paragraph supports your position, it does not undermine it

**Conclusion (40–60 words)**
- Restate your position in new words
- Summarise your two main ideas in one sentence each
- No new information

**Total: 270–320 words. Written in 38–40 minutes.**

The logic that connects these paragraphs — not the discourse markers that label them — is what produces Band 7 Coherence and Cohesion.

## How Indian Candidates Should Approach Writing Task 1

Writing Task 1 is worth one-third of the Writing module score — less than Task 2, but not negligible.

For **IELTS Academic** (the version most Indian Express Entry and UK visa applicants take): Task 1 requires you to describe a graph, chart, table, map or diagram in at least 150 words.

The most common error for Indian candidates on Task 1 is **over-interpretation** — drawing conclusions or speculating about reasons for trends when the task only requires description. Academic Task 1 asks you to report what you see, not explain why it happened.

The second most common error is **copying data without summarising**. The examiner wants an overview — the most significant trends and comparisons — before the specific data. Starting with data and never stepping back to the big picture consistently loses marks on Task Achievement for Task 1.

**The formula that works:**

*Introduction (paraphrase) → Overview (the two or three most significant things shown) → Body Paragraph 1 (specific data with comparisons) → Body Paragraph 2 (specific data with comparisons)*

No conclusion for Task 1. The overview serves that function.
