**You write well. Everyone tells you so. Your WhatsApp messages are articulate. Your work emails are professional. You scored high in English all through secondary school and university. And you are still getting Band 6 on IELTS Writing Task 2.**

**This is not a coincidence. It is a pattern. And it has a specific name.**

Of all the nationalities sitting IELTS globally, Nigerian candidates have one of the sharpest gaps between their actual English ability and their IELTS Writing score.

Nigerian English — shaped by decades of British colonial education, strong oral tradition, religious oratory, and a deeply literate culture — produces writers who are expressive, confident, and often sophisticated. Walk into any Nigerian university, law firm, hospital or government office and you will find people writing English that would hold up in any context.

And yet. Band 6 on Writing Task 2. Again and again.

The reason is not your English. The reason is what IELTS is specifically measuring — and the specific pattern that Nigerian academic English education has trained you into without telling you.

## What IELTS Writing Task 2 Is Actually Measuring

This is the section most Nigerian candidates skip because they assume they already know. Read it anyway. The specific details here are what change scores.

IELTS Writing Task 2 is marked against four equally-weighted criteria. Each criterion is worth exactly 25 percent of your Writing Task 2 score. Your overall Writing Task 2 band is the average of all four.

### The Four Band Descriptor Criteria — What They Mean and What Band 7 Requires

**Task Achievement (25%)**
Are you answering the question fully? Is your position clear, consistent and developed throughout the entire essay?

At Band 7, the position is clear and developed and the main ideas are extended and supported — though supporting detail may drift toward over-generalisation.

**Coherence and Cohesion (25%)**
Does your argument flow logically? Are ideas connected skilfully through varied means — not just labelled with discourse markers?

At Band 7, information is organised logically with a clear overall progression, and cohesive devices are used with flexibility rather than mechanically.

**Lexical Resource (25%)**
How wide and accurate is your vocabulary? Are you using less common words correctly?

At Band 7, the vocabulary range allows some flexibility and precision, less common words appear with some awareness of style and collocation, and errors in word choice, spelling or word formation are only occasional.

**Grammatical Range and Accuracy (25%)**
How varied and accurate are your sentence structures?

At Band 7, a variety of complex structures appear, error-free sentences are frequent, and grammar and punctuation are well controlled with only a few errors.

Now look at that list again with Nigerian candidate performance in mind:

- **Task Achievement** — Nigerian candidates typically score 6–7. Most Nigerian essays answer the question and develop a position. This is not the primary gap.
- **Lexical Resource** — Nigerian candidates typically score 6–7. Vocabulary is a strength. This is not the primary gap.
- **Grammatical Range** — Nigerian candidates typically score 5.5–7. Grammar is generally solid. This is a secondary gap for some candidates.
- **Coherence and Cohesion** — Nigerian candidates consistently score **5–6**. This is the gap. This is what is pulling your Writing Task 2 score below Band 7.

A Band 5 on any single criterion drags the overall Writing Task 2 score to Band 6 regardless of how strong the other three are. Your Band 6.0 or 6.5 is not a general Writing failure. It is a Coherence and Cohesion failure that is pulling down a Writing profile that could be Band 7.

## Why Nigerian Candidates Consistently Lose Marks on Coherence and Cohesion

This is the part that requires honesty about Nigerian academic English education specifically.

Nigerian secondary and university essay writing is taught around a structure that is clear, logical and well-organised:

*Introduction → Firstly, point one → Secondly, point two → Furthermore, point three → In addition, point four → In conclusion, summary*

This structure is taught from JSS1 through to university dissertations. It produces essays that are organised, legible and easy to follow. In Nigerian academic assessment, it scores well — because Nigerian academic assessment rewards organisation and content.

IELTS assessment rewards something different at Band 7 and above. It rewards **flexibility** in how ideas are connected — not just the presence of organisation.

The Band 6 Coherence descriptor describes a writer who uses cohesive devices effectively but whose cohesion within or between sentences can be faulty or mechanical.

The word **mechanical** is the key. Mechanical is precisely what Firstly, Secondly, Furthermore, In conclusion produces when used as the primary — or only — cohesive tool.

An IELTS examiner reading their fiftieth essay of the day, seeing *"Firstly, it is important to note… Secondly, one must consider… Furthermore, it should be emphasised… In conclusion, it is evident that…"* — they have read this structure in every band 5–6 essay that day. It signals a template. It signals that the writer knows how to organise but has not demonstrated the flexible command of cohesion that distinguishes a Band 7 writer.

The cruellest part of this for Nigerian candidates is that the template they were taught — the one that served them through school and university — is the exact thing costing them their band on IELTS.

## The Nigerian Writing Profile in Detail

Beyond the primary Coherence and Cohesion gap, there are two secondary patterns specific to Nigerian IELTS candidates that are worth understanding.

### Secondary Pattern 1: The Rhetorical Opening

Nigerian academic and professional writing often opens with a broad, dramatic statement:

*"Since the beginning of time, human beings have always faced the challenge of…"*

*"It is an undeniable fact that in today's rapidly changing world…"*

*"The issue of [topic] has become increasingly prevalent in modern society…"*

These openings are not wrong. They are weak. They are so common in Band 5–6 essays that experienced examiners recognise them as signals of a generic response. They waste your first sentence — the sentence that should be paraphrasing the question with precision and signalling to the examiner that you have understood what is being asked.

**The fix:** Your introduction should open with a direct paraphrase of the question topic — not a broad statement about humanity, society or time. Then state your position. Then preview your two main ideas. Nothing else.

### Secondary Pattern 2: Arguing Without Committing

Nigerian essay writing education often values presenting multiple perspectives — the "on one hand, on the other hand" approach that demonstrates breadth of consideration. In Nigerian academic contexts, this balanced exploration is rewarded.

In IELTS Task 2, it is penalised.

When a question says *"To what extent do you agree or disagree?"* — the examiner expects a clear, consistent position. Not a balanced exploration. Not "there are arguments on both sides." A position: I agree to a large extent, for these two specific reasons.

Nigerian candidates who write genuinely balanced essays — giving equal weight to both sides without committing — consistently lose Task Achievement marks because their position is unclear or inconsistent across the essay.

**The fix:** Before you start writing, decide your position. Write it in one sentence. Every paragraph should support that position. If you want to acknowledge the other side, do it in one concession sentence — then return immediately to your main argument.

## The Three Fixes: Specific, Actionable, Measurable

### Fix 1: The Discourse Marker Ban (Weeks 1–2)

For your next three Task 2 essays, these words are banned:

- ❌ Firstly / Secondly / Thirdly
- ❌ Furthermore / Moreover / In addition
- ❌ However (as a sentence opener)
- ❌ In conclusion / To conclude / To summarise / In summary

You must connect your ideas without them.

**What to use instead:**

<div class="table-wrap">

| Instead of... | Use... |
|---------------|--------|
| Firstly, | [Start with your idea directly] |
| Furthermore, | This [noun] also... / The same pattern... / A further dimension... |
| However, | [Embed the contrast mid-clause: "While X is true, Y..."] |
| In conclusion, | [Restate your position using different words — no signal needed] |
| Secondly, | [Use a pronoun or synonym that links to the previous idea] |

</div>

The first essay without these words will feel uncomfortable. You will feel as though your essay has no structure. Read it back — you will find it flows more naturally than your previous essays because the ideas connect through logic rather than labels.

After three essays, reintroduce discourse markers selectively — one per paragraph maximum, varied across the essay.

### Fix 2: The Backwards Paragraph Check (Every Essay)

After you finish every Task 2 essay, read it backwards — paragraph by paragraph.

Start at your conclusion. Read it. Ask: *does this follow logically from the body paragraph above it?*

Read your final body paragraph. Ask: *does this follow logically from the body paragraph above it?*

Continue to your introduction.

At every paragraph boundary, ask the same question. If the answer is ever "not really" or "the connection is not clear" — you have found a coherence gap. The ideas in those paragraphs are adjacent but not connected. Fix the connection and your Coherence score improves.

This check takes three minutes. Do it every time.

### Fix 3: The Synonym Chain (Before Every Essay)

Before you begin writing, take 90 seconds to map your key nouns:

**Example — essay topic:** *The rise of social media has changed how people communicate. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages.*

<div class="table-wrap">

| Key noun | Synonyms and related phrases |
|----------|------------------------------|
| social media | digital platforms, online networks, these technologies |
| communication | interaction, exchange of information, how people connect |
| advantages | benefits, positive effects, opportunities |
| disadvantages | drawbacks, challenges, negative consequences |

</div>

Use these chains throughout your essay — not repeating the same noun but cycling through synonyms. This does two things simultaneously: it demonstrates Lexical Resource variety and it creates Cohesion through reference. Two criteria improved with one technique.

## Band 6 vs Band 7: The Same Essay at Two Levels

Here is the same argument written at Band 6 and Band 7 Coherence level. The ideas are identical. Only the cohesion changes.

### Band 6 Version — The Nigerian Template

*"There are several advantages of working from home. Firstly, employees are able to save time that would have been spent commuting to the office. Secondly, working from home allows workers to have a better work-life balance. Furthermore, companies can significantly reduce their overhead costs by allowing staff to work remotely. In conclusion, remote work has many benefits for both employees and employers."*

**What the examiner sees:** Organised but mechanical. Every paragraph labelled, not connected. No progression — just a list of points separated by structural markers. The argument does not build. It accumulates.

### Band 7 Version — Connected and Progressive

*"Remote work has reshaped the daily experience of employment in ways that benefit workers and organisations alike. Employees who previously spent hours in transit can redirect that time toward productive work — an efficiency gain that research consistently links to higher output and lower stress. This benefit extends beyond the individual: when companies reduce their physical office footprint, operational costs fall proportionally, freeing resources for investment rather than overhead. What emerged as a temporary response to circumstance has become, for many organisations, a structural advantage they are unwilling to abandon."*

**What the examiner sees:** Ideas connected through pronouns (this benefit, that time), synonyms (transit for commuting, operational costs for overhead), and logical progression — each sentence builds on the one before it. No discourse markers. No labels. The argument develops rather than lists.

The vocabulary is not dramatically more sophisticated. The grammar is not dramatically more complex. The Coherence is entirely different. And Coherence is worth 25 percent of the score.

## The Writing Task 2 Structure That Consistently Scores Band 7 for Nigerian Candidates

This is not a template. A template is what is keeping you at Band 6. This is a logic structure — the difference matters.

**Introduction (50–70 words)**
- Paraphrase the question topic in your own words — never copy the question wording
- State your clear position on the question — not "there are arguments on both sides" but a specific stance
- Preview the two main ideas that will support your position

**Body Paragraph 1 (80–100 words)**
- State your first main idea in your own words
- Explain the mechanism — why this idea is true, not just that it is
- Give one specific, concrete example — real or plausible
- Connect back to your overall position

**Body Paragraph 2 (80–100 words)**
- State your second main idea
- Explain and develop it
- Give one specific example
- Connect logically to Body Paragraph 1 — use a pronoun or synonym that references the previous idea

**Concession paragraph (optional, 50–70 words)**
- Acknowledge the opposing view in one or two sentences
- Immediately undercut it with a specific counter-reason
- This paragraph supports your position — it does not undermine it

**Conclusion (40–60 words)**
- Restate your position in completely new words
- Summarise your two main ideas in one sentence each
- No new information. No "In conclusion."

**Total: 270–320 words. 40 minutes. Written to Band 7.**

## Writing Task 1: What Nigerian Candidates Need to Know

Writing Task 1 is worth one-third of the Writing module. It is not negligible.

**For IELTS Academic** (most Nigerian Express Entry and UK visa applicants): Task 1 requires describing a graph, chart, table, map or diagram in at least 150 words. The two most common Nigerian candidate errors:

**Error 1: Over-interpretation.** Nigerian academic writing often analyses and draws conclusions. Academic Task 1 asks you to describe what you see — not explain why, not draw conclusions, not speculate. Report. Only.

**Error 2: Missing the overview.** The most important paragraph in Academic Task 1 is the overview — the two or three most significant trends or comparisons shown in the data, stated before any specific numbers. Many Nigerian candidates dive directly into data without stepping back to state what the data shows overall. This costs Task Achievement marks every time.

**For IELTS General Training** (also accepted for most Canadian immigration pathways): Task 1 is a letter — formal, semi-formal or informal depending on the prompt. Nigerian candidates typically perform well on Task 1 General because letter-writing is a familiar format. The most common error is writing in the wrong register — formal language in a letter that specifies an informal situation, or casual language in a letter that should be addressed to a company or authority.
