**There is a difference between the IELTS score that makes you eligible for Express Entry and the IELTS score that actually gets you an Invitation to Apply. Most Indian applicants are aiming for the wrong number.**

India sends more Express Entry applicants than almost any other country in the world. And yet a significant proportion of Indian candidates sit in the Express Entry pool for months — or years — without receiving an Invitation to Apply (ITA). Their profiles are eligible. Their IELTS scores meet the minimum. But they are not getting invited.

The reason, in the majority of cases, comes down to one thing: their IELTS score is producing a CRS total that falls below recent draw cut-offs.

This article explains exactly what IELTS score you need — not to be eligible, but to be invited. The two numbers are very different.

This guide assumes IELTS. IRCC also accepts CELPIP, but [for applicants testing in India, IELTS is almost always the practical choice](/ielts-vs-celpip-which-should-you-take/).

  <strong>The short version</strong>
  <ul>
    <li>Minimum for eligibility is CLB 7 (about Band 6.0 in every skill). It gets you into the pool, not invited.</li>

    <li>What actually produces an invitation: a Provincial Nominee Program nomination (+600), a job offer, Canadian experience via CEC, or a category-based draw (STEM, healthcare) with a lower cut-off.</li>

## How Express Entry Actually Works for Indian Applicants

Express Entry is a points-based system. IRCC (Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada) manages a pool of eligible candidates and issues Invitations to Apply through regular draws. The draws select candidates above a CRS (Comprehensive Ranking System) cut-off score. If your CRS is below the cut-off, you are not invited — regardless of your qualifications or intentions.

The CRS score is built from several factors:

<div class="table-wrap">

| CRS Factor | Maximum Points (Single Applicant) |
|------------|----------------------------------|
| Age | 110 |
| Education (Canadian) | 150 |
| Education (Foreign) | 140 |
| First official language (IELTS) | 136 |
| Second official language | 24 |
| Canadian work experience | 80 |
| Foreign work experience | 50 |
| Arranged employment | 200 |
| Provincial nomination | 600 |
| Adaptability and other factors | 100 |

</div>

Language — your IELTS score — is worth up to **136 CRS points for a single applicant.** That is the single largest factor in your CRS score that you can actively improve after your profile is created.

Age, education and work experience are largely fixed. Your IELTS score is not.

## The Number Every Indian Applicant Needs to Understand

### Minimum vs Competitive

**Minimum for eligibility (Federal Skilled Worker):**
CLB 7 across all four skills = approximately Band 6.0 in all four skills

This gets you into the pool. It does not get you invited.

**Competitive for most draws:**
CLB 9 across all four skills = Band 7.0 in all four skills

At CLB 9, a typical Indian applicant with a foreign bachelor's degree and three years of skilled work experience has a CRS score in the range of 430–480. Federal Skilled Worker draw cut-offs in 2024–2025 have ranged from 491 to 549. Most Indian applicants at CLB 9 are still below recent cut-offs without additional factors.

**What changes the picture:**
CLB 10 across all four skills = Band 7.5–8.0

At CLB 10, first-official-language points rise to roughly 128 across the four skills, compared with about 116 at CLB 9. The skill-transferability bonuses that pair a strong language score with your education and work experience widen the real gap further — run your exact profile through the [Canada PR points calculator](/tools/canada-pr-points-calculator/) or the official IRCC CRS tool. For applicants sitting just below a draw cut-off, this is often the improvement that brings the CRS into invitation range.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Skill improved from 7.0 to 8.0 | Additional CRS Points |
|--------------------------------|----------------------|

*Note: this represents the difference in per-skill points between CLB 9 and CLB 10 for first language only. **The practical implication:** Moving from Band 7.0 to Band 8.0 across all four skills adds approximately 12 additional CRS points from language improvement alone. For many Indian applicants, that difference — combined with other profile factors — is what crosses the invitation threshold.

## IELTS Requirements by NOC Category for Indian Express Entry Applicants

Different Express Entry streams have different language requirements. Indian applicants apply across all three streams, and the requirements differ.

### Federal Skilled Worker (FSW) — Most Common for Indian Applicants

<div class="table-wrap">

| Requirement | Minimum | Competitive |
|-------------|---------|-------------|
| IELTS Overall | Band 6.0 (CLB 7) | Band 7.0–8.0 (CLB 9–10) |
| Per skill minimum | Band 5.5 | Band 7.0–8.0 |
| NOC requirement | TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 | Any eligible NOC |

</div>

Most Indian IT professionals, engineers, accountants, doctors and managers apply through FSW. The minimum CLB 7 makes them eligible. CLB 9 or above makes them competitive.

### Canadian Experience Class (CEC) — For Indians on Work Permits in Canada

<div class="table-wrap">

| NOC Category | Minimum CLB | IELTS Equivalent |
|--------------|-------------|-----------------|
| TEER 0 or 1 | CLB 7 | Band 6.0 per skill |
| TEER 2 or 3 | CLB 5 | Band 5.0 per skill |

</div>

CEC applicants typically have higher CRS scores due to Canadian work experience points. However, language still contributes significantly — improving from CLB 7 to CLB 9 adds approximately 92 CRS points for a TEER 1 CEC candidate.

### Federal Skilled Trades (FST) — Trades Workers

<div class="table-wrap">

| Requirement | Minimum CLB |
|-------------|-------------|
| Listening | CLB 5 |
| Speaking | CLB 5 |
| Reading | CLB 4 |
| Writing | CLB 4 |

</div>

Trades workers have lower language requirements for eligibility. However, higher language scores still add CRS points and improve invitation prospects.

### What Actually Gets Indian Applicants Invited

For most Indian FSW applicants without Canadian experience or a job offer, an Express Entry invitation typically requires one or more of:

**1. Provincial Nominee Program (PNP) nomination**
A provincial nomination adds 600 CRS points — effectively guaranteeing an invitation in the next draw. This is the most reliable route for most Indian applicants without a dominant CRS profile.

**2. Canadian job offer**
A valid LMIA-supported job offer adds 50–200 CRS points depending on the NOC level.

**3. Canadian work experience (CEC)**
Moving to Canada on a work permit, gaining Canadian experience, and applying through CEC typically produces a significantly higher CRS than FSW.

**4. Language score at CLB 10 or above**
For Indian applicants close to the cut-off, improving IELTS from Band 7.0 to Band 8.0 can close the remaining gap — particularly in category-based draws where lower CRS scores are invited.

## Category-Based Draws: The Opportunity Most Indian Applicants Are Missing

Since 2023, IRCC has been running category-based draws that select candidates from specific occupational categories — not just the overall pool. These draws often have lower CRS cut-offs than general FSW draws.

**Categories that frequently appear in draws:**

<div class="table-wrap">

| Category | Who Qualifies | Typical Cut-off vs General FSW |
|----------|--------------|-------------------------------|
| STEM occupations | IT, engineers, scientists | 10–40 points lower |
| Healthcare occupations | Doctors, nurses, pharmacists | 15–50 points lower |
| Trades occupations | Electricians, plumbers, welders | 20–60 points lower |
| French language proficiency | CLB 7 in French | Significantly lower |
| Agriculture and agri-food | Farmers, food processors | 20–50 points lower |

</div>

**For Indian IT professionals and engineers:** The STEM category draws are significant. An Indian software engineer at Band 7.0 with a strong CRS might be below the general FSW cut-off but above the STEM category cut-off in the same draw period.

**The French language pathway:** If an Indian applicant has both English (IELTS) and French (TEF or TCF) language scores, French language points add up to 24 additional CRS points — and French-language category draws have much lower cut-offs. For Indian applicants willing to invest in French language preparation, this is a viable CRS improvement strategy.

## The IELTS Score That Changes Everything for Indian Applicants

Given the complete picture above, here is the honest answer to what IELTS score Indian applicants need:

**For eligibility:** Band 6.0 across all skills (CLB 7)

**For a competitive standard FSW profile:** Band 7.5–8.0 (CLB 10) across all skills, combined with other strong CRS factors

**For category-based draws (STEM, healthcare):** Band 7.0 (CLB 9) may be sufficient if your occupational category draw cut-off is lower than the general FSW cut-off

**The most important insight:** For the majority of Indian applicants, IELTS improvement from Band 7.0 to Band 8.0 is the most actionable CRS improvement available — because age, education and foreign work experience are largely fixed, while language score can be improved in 4–12 weeks of targeted preparation.

## How Indian Applicants Improve From Band 7.0 to Band 8.0

The gap between Band 7.0 and Band 8.0 is not as large as it sounds. For most Indian applicants, it comes down to two or three specific sub-skills rather than a fundamental English ability difference — and a structured runway like our [30-day IELTS preparation plan](/ielts-preparation-plan-30-days-india) is usually enough time to close it.

**Common gaps for Indian applicants targeting Band 8.0:**

*Listening:* Section 4 accuracy. Most Indian candidates score well on Sections 1–3 but lose marks on Section 4 — the academic monologue. Improving Section 4 from Band 7.0 to Band 8.0 typically takes three to four weeks of targeted practice with the trigger keyword technique.

*Reading:* True/False/Not Given precision. Most Indian candidates have strong Reading but lose specific marks on T/F/NG. The Not Given distinction — absence of information is not contradiction — is the specific rule that moves Reading from 7.0 to 7.5 or 8.0.

*Writing:* Coherence and Cohesion. Moving from Band 7.0 to Band 7.5 on Writing requires the argument to flow with greater flexibility — not just logically, but with varied cohesive devices and smooth paragraph transitions that do not rely on discourse markers. [Our full breakdown of the Band 7 Writing Task 2 fix](/ielts-writing-task-2-band-7-india) covers this in detail.

*Speaking:* Fluency. Moving from Band 7.0 to Band 8.0 on Speaking requires eliminating hesitation patterns and filler words (um, uh, like, you know). Most Indian candidates have strong Speaking content — the gap is in delivery smoothness.
