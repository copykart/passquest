Every year, hundreds of thousands of Indian professionals submit Express Entry profiles hoping for an Invitation to Apply for Canadian permanent residence. Almost all of them need to demonstrate English language proficiency. Two tests are accepted: IELTS and CELPIP.

## The One Thing Most IELTS vs CELPIP Articles Get Wrong

**CELPIP does not have test centres in India.**

As of early 2026, CELPIP General test centres are located primarily in Canada, with a small number of international locations. India is not among them. This means that for an Indian applicant to take CELPIP, they must travel internationally — to the Middle East, Southeast Asia, or another CELPIP-approved location — at significant cost and logistical complexity.

IELTS, by contrast, has over 1,000 test sessions annually across India — in Delhi, Mumbai, Bangalore, Chennai, Hyderabad, Pune, Kolkata and dozens of smaller cities. You can typically book an IELTS test within two to four weeks from any major Indian city.

For the overwhelming majority of Indian applicants, **IELTS is the only practical choice.** The rest of this article explains the complete picture so you can make an informed decision — and prepares you to score exactly what you need.

## What Is IELTS? What Is CELPIP?

### IELTS (International English Language Testing System)

Jointly owned by the British Council, IDP Education and Cambridge Assessment English. Available in two versions:

- **IELTS Academic** — for university admissions and skilled migration
- **IELTS General Training** — for work experience, secondary education, and most immigration pathways including Canada Express Entry

Both versions test the same four skills: Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking. The Listening and Speaking components are identical across both. Reading and Writing content differs — Academic uses more complex texts and requires a data description for Task 1. General Training uses everyday texts and requires a letter for Task 1.

**For Canada Express Entry:** IELTS General Training is accepted and is what most applicants choose. IELTS Academic is also accepted.

### CELPIP (Canadian English Language Proficiency Index Program)

Developed and administered by Paragon Testing Enterprises, a subsidiary of the University of British Columbia. Available in two versions:

- **CELPIP General** — for Canadian immigration purposes
- **CELPIP General LS** — only Listening and Speaking, used for Canadian citizenship applications

CELPIP is entirely computer-based. There is no paper option and no handwritten component. The exam simulates real Canadian English situations — listening to voicemails, writing emails, discussing everyday scenarios. It is explicitly Canadian in its vocabulary and context.

**For Canada Express Entry:** CELPIP General is accepted by IRCC as a designated language test.

## How IELTS Scores Convert to CLB for Express Entry

Canada's immigration system uses Canadian Language Benchmarks (CLB) — not IELTS bands directly. Your IELTS score is converted to a CLB level for each skill, and those CLB levels determine your CRS points.

### IELTS to CLB Conversion Table

<div class="table-wrap">

| IELTS Band (per skill) | CLB Level |
|------------|-----------|
| 6.0 | CLB 7 |
| 6.5 | CLB 8 |
| 7.0 | CLB 9 |
| 7.5 | CLB 10 |
| 8.0 | CLB 10 |
| 8.5 | CLB 11 |
| 9.0 | CLB 12 |

</div>

*Each CLB level above the minimum adds CRS points, and the exact points depend on your full profile (age, education, spouse factors and more). Run your own numbers in the [Canada PR points calculator](/tools/canada-pr-points-calculator/) or the official IRCC CRS tool.*

### What This Means Practically for Indian Express Entry Applicants

**Band 7.0 across all four skills (CLB 9):**
This is the minimum that makes you competitive in most Federal Skilled Worker draws. You meet the language requirement and receive the baseline CRS language points.

**Band 7.5–8.0 across all four skills (CLB 10):**
Adds a further block of CRS points on top of the CLB 9 baseline across all four skills. That difference can be the gap between waiting years for an invitation and receiving one within months.

**The practical implication:**
For most Indian applicants, the difference between Band 7.0 and Band 8.0 on IELTS is not a matter of English ability. It is a matter of exam preparation precision. Indian professionals who speak excellent English in their daily work and careers routinely score below their actual English level on IELTS because they are unfamiliar with the specific format, the marking criteria, and the question types that cost points.

## CELPIP vs IELTS: Which Is Harder for Indian Applicants?

This is a genuine question that deserves an honest answer rather than a diplomatic non-answer.

**CELPIP tends to be considered slightly easier by test takers who:**
- Are comfortable with computers and typing (the entire exam is computer-based)
- Are familiar with Canadian English vocabulary and everyday Canadian situations
- Find formal academic writing (IELTS Task 1 data description) challenging
- Are comfortable with speaking to a computer rather than a human examiner

**IELTS tends to be considered slightly more manageable for Indian applicants who:**
- Are more comfortable with structured academic English (which most Indian university graduates are)
- Prefer a face-to-face Speaking component with a human examiner
- Want the option of a paper-based exam
- Have studied British English throughout their education — which the majority of Indian candidates have

**The honest answer:** For most Indian applicants, IELTS is both more accessible and more familiar. The academic English register of IELTS aligns more closely with the English education most Indian candidates received. And given that CELPIP is not available in India, the question is largely academic.

## The Hidden Strategic Factor: Test Flexibility

One factor that almost no comparison article covers is **what happens if you need to resit.**

IELTS can be resat as often as you like, at hundreds of centres across India, typically within two to four weeks of booking. If you score 6.5 when you needed 7.0, you can resit within a month.

CELPIP — for an Indian applicant — requires international travel every time you sit the exam. A resit means booking flights, accommodation and a test slot abroad. This is not just expensive; it adds months to your timeline.

For a process as time-sensitive as Express Entry — where CRS cut-offs shift with every draw and profile validity is two years — the ability to resit quickly and locally is a significant strategic advantage.

## How Indian Professionals Typically Score on IELTS for Canada PR

Based on typical profiles of Indian Express Entry applicants:

**Software engineers and IT professionals:**
Reading and Writing tend to be strong. Listening Section 4 (academic monologue) and Speaking Fluency are the typical weak points. Target: Band 7.0–8.0.

**Healthcare professionals (doctors, pharmacists):**
Strong across all skills, but Writing Task 1 (data description for Academic) requires specific practice. Target: Band 7.0–7.5 for most provincial requirements.

**Finance and accounting professionals:**
Strong Reading and Writing. Listening Section 4 and Speaking Part 3 (abstract discussion) are typically the gaps. Target: Band 7.0.

**Engineers (non-IT):**
Variable. Technical English is strong. Formal academic writing register can be unfamiliar. Listening Section 4 is the most common gap. Target: Band 7.0–7.5.
