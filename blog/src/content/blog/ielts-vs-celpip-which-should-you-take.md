If your goal is Canada — study, work or permanent residence — you will eventually have to prove your English. The two tests that matter are **IELTS** and **CELPIP**, and they are more different than they first appear.

<div class="tldr">
  <strong>Quick answer</strong>
  <ul>
    <li>Applying to Canada and testing <em>inside</em> Canada? CELPIP is worth a look — it's fast, all-computer, and uses a familiar accent.</li>
    <li>Testing outside Canada, or applying anywhere else? Take IELTS — CELPIP centres are scarce outside Canada.</li>
    <li>Both are accepted by IRCC for express-entry and PR.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

## The formats are not alike

IELTS gives you a paper or computer option and splits the test across two settings: Listening, Reading and Writing in one sitting, then a face-to-face Speaking interview with an examiner (sometimes on a different day). CELPIP is one 3-hour computer session, Speaking included — you talk to a microphone, not a person.

<div class="table-wrap">

| | IELTS (General Training) | CELPIP-General |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery | Paper or computer | Computer only |
| Speaking | Live examiner | Recorded, to a microphone |
| Total time | ~2h 45m + interview | ~3 hours, one sitting |
| Results | 3–13 days | 3–4 business days |
| Accent | Mostly British/Australian | North American |

</div>

## Scoring maps differently

IELTS reports **band scores** from 1 to 9 per skill. CELPIP reports **levels** from 1 to 12. Canadian immigration converts both to the same Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB), so what matters is the CLB your program needs.

- CLB 9 ≈ IELTS 7.0 reading / 6.0 listening-writing-speaking varies, or CELPIP 9 across the board.
- Aim for the CLB in your program's rules, then add a half-band of headroom.

<div class="callout">
  <strong>Watch the skill split</strong>
  <p>Express Entry scores each skill separately. One weak band can cost you more points than a strong average earns — practise your weakest skill first.</p>
</div>

## Availability is the deciding factor for many

CELPIP is run by Paragon Testing and is widely available across Canada, with a growing but still limited set of international centres. IELTS runs in 140+ countries. If you are testing from Nigeria, India, the Philippines or most of Europe, IELTS is usually the only practical option.

## So which one?

Take **CELPIP** if you are in Canada, comfortable typing under time pressure, and prefer a North American accent. Take **IELTS** if you are outside Canada, want a paper option, or might use the score for a country other than Canada.
